## Recommendation

**Plate Tectonics**

Plate Tectonics is the strongest bounded next increment after Water Cycle,
Latitude and Longitude, and Atmosphere. It adds a causal Earth-surface model
without repeating the prior packages' primary emphasis. It can demonstrate
mechanism/process, chronology, comparison of boundary types, spatial
relationships, evidence/case-study semantics, and carefully scoped
cause/effect using the existing universal model.

## Expected conceptual scope

The future package should be limited to:

- the basic plate-tectonic model;
- lithosphere and asthenosphere as necessary prerequisites;
- divergent, convergent, and transform boundaries;
- relative plate motion and associated broad surface effects;
- a small number of authoritative, clearly scoped examples.

It should not attempt a complete geology, mineralogy, earthquake, volcano, or
mountain-building curriculum.

## Likely source families

- U.S. Geological Survey and other national geological surveys
- NOAA or equivalent official Earth-science agencies where relevant
- NASA Earth science resources
- authoritative university geology departments
- peer-reviewed geoscience references for non-basic or contested claims

Source selection should prefer claim-specific official locators over broad
landing pages.

## Anticipated ContentBlock patterns

Likely useful types include definition, explanation, mechanism-process,
comparison, chronology, cause-effect, example or case-study, misconception,
and exception. Formula or exam-note blocks should be used only if the actual
content requires them; no block type should be added for coverage alone.

## Semantic challenges and risks

1. Distinguishing the plate-tectonic model from individual hypotheses and
   historical development.
2. Avoiding the misconception that every boundary produces the same landform
   or hazard.
3. Keeping lithosphere/asthenosphere terminology accurate and appropriately
   introductory.
4. Separating broad causal relationships from deterministic predictions.
5. Avoiding unsupported claims about rates, dates, or specific events.
6. Preventing scope creep into a complete physical-geography curriculum.

## Why this is appropriate as Batch #4

Plate Tectonics expands the demonstrated canonical set from hydrologic
processes, spatial coordinates, and atmospheric structure to a bounded
geoscience explanatory model. It tests whether the universal schema handles
causal mechanisms, boundary comparisons, chronology, and evidence without
schema changes or Geography-specific fields. It also has strong foundational
and BCS relevance and a mature authoritative source ecosystem.
